\textbf{Background:}
Pain reassessment is a core component of emergency care, yet factors associated with the timing of repeat pain assessment remain understudied compared with analgesic treatment outcomes.

\textbf{Methods:}
We conducted a retrospective cohort study of emergency department (ED) encounters with documented pain assessments using the MIMIC-IV Emergency Department database.
The primary outcome was time from the initial documented pain score to the first subsequent reassessment.
We fit Cox proportional hazards models with sequential adjustment for clinical presentation (M1), demographic and social factors (M2), illness severity (M3), and ED workflow context (M4).
Sensitivity analyses included within--acuity models, severe-pain subsets, post-analgesic reassessment timing, and temporal trend analyses.

\textbf{Results:}
Among \CohortN{} encounters with a documented reassessment (\CohortEvents{} events), clinical presentation and workflow factors were strongly associated with reassessment timing.
In the primary adjusted model (M4), higher initial pain (HR \HRPainMfour{}) and ambulance arrival (HR \HRAmbulanceMfour{}) were associated with faster reassessment, whereas lower triage acuity---higher Emergency Severity Index (ESI) level---was associated with slower reassessment (HR \HRESIMfour{}).
Medicaid insurance was associated with slower reassessment compared with private insurance (HR \HRMedicaidMfour{}), with minimal attenuation across sequential models.
Race and ethnicity associations were smaller and attenuated after adjustment for severity and workflow.
No meaningful secular trend in reassessment was observed (HR per 5-year increase \HRYearFive{}).

\textbf{Conclusions:}
Pain reassessment timing reflects a combination of clinical presentation and operational ED factors.
Insurance-based differences persisted after multivariable adjustment and within triage-acuity strata, suggesting a disparity in monitoring intensity that warrants further quality-improvement attention.
