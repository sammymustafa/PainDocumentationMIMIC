\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Cohort characteristics and descriptive outcomes}

The analytic cohort included \CohortN{} ED encounters; \CohortEvents{} (56.3\%) had at least one documented pain reassessment during the stay (Table~\ref{tab:cohort}).
The mean age was 56.2 years (SD 19.7); 70.4\% of patients were White, 19.7\% Black, 6.9\% Hispanic, and 3.1\% Asian.
Most encounters were trauma-related (92.8\%); acute pancreatitis accounted for 7.2\%.
Insurance distribution included Medicare, private, Medicaid, undocumented (missing payer in EHR), and uninsured (self-pay); see Table~\ref{tab:cohort}.
Mean initial pain score was 6.1 (SD 2.6); mean ESI was 2.7 (SD 0.7).

Reassessment was often delayed: the median time to first reassessment was 185 minutes (IQR 96--311).
Only 9.6\% of patients were reassessed within 60 minutes and 21.6\% within 120 minutes.
Unadjusted Kaplan--Meier curves showed separation by insurance and race/ethnicity (Figure~\ref{fig:km}); 60-minute reassessment rates varied by pain severity stratum (Figure~\ref{fig:rates60}).

\subsection{Primary adjusted model: clinical severity and workflow}

In the primary M4 Cox model (Figure~\ref{fig:m4forest}), clinical presentation and ED workflow factors were strongly associated with reassessment timing.
Each one-point increase in initial pain score was associated with faster reassessment (HR \HRPainMfour{}).
Higher ESI level (lower acuity) was associated with slower reassessment (HR \HRESIMfour{}).
Ambulance arrival (vs.\ walk-in) and night shift (vs.\ day) were associated with faster reassessment (HR \HRAmbulanceMfour{} and HR \HRNightMfour{}, respectively).
Weekend arrival and ED crowding variables showed weaker or null associations at conventional significance thresholds.

\subsection{Insurance: strongest and most persistent disparity signal}

Insurance showed the largest and most robust associations with reassessment timing.
Compared with private insurance, Medicaid was associated with substantially slower reassessment in M4 (HR \HRMedicaidMfour{}).
Medicare was also associated with slower reassessment (HR \HRMedicareMfour{}).
Undocumented insurance (missing payer documentation) had a hazard ratio near unity versus private (HR \HRUndocumentedMfour{}).

Sequential attenuation demonstrated minimal change in the Medicaid association from M2 (\HRMedicaidMtwo{}) through M3 (\HRMedicaidMthree{}) to M4 (\HRMedicaidMfourSeq{}; Figure~\ref{fig:attenuation}).
An insurance-focused analysis including Kaplan--Meier curves, within-acuity estimates, and pain-stratum models corroborated this pattern (Figure~\ref{fig:insurance}).

\subsection{Race and ethnicity: smaller effects with attenuation}

Race and ethnicity associations were generally smaller than insurance and attenuated with sequential adjustment.
Black patients had slower reassessment in M2 (HR \HRBlackMtwo{}) that moved toward the null in M4 (HR \HRBlackMfour{}; Figure~\ref{fig:attenuation}).
Hispanic patients showed no meaningful association after full adjustment (HR \HRHispanicMfour{}).
Asian patients had slower reassessment in M4 (HR \HRAsianMfour{}), but estimates were less stable in severe-pain sensitivity analyses (wide confidence intervals).

\subsection{Within-acuity and severe-pain sensitivity analyses}

Within-acuity models (Figure~\ref{fig:withinacuity}) suggested that insurance associations were most pronounced at higher acuity.
Among ESI 1--2 encounters, Medicaid was associated with slower reassessment (HR \HRMedicaidESI12{}).
Among ESI 3 encounters, Medicaid remained associated with slower reassessment (HR \HRMedicaidESI3{}).
Estimates in ESI 4--5 were imprecise and directionally unstable, reflecting smaller event counts.

In severe-pain sensitivity analyses (initial pain 7--10; Figure~\ref{fig:severe}), patterns were similar to the primary cohort: Medicaid HR \HRMedicaidSevere{} and Black vs.\ White HR \HRBlackSevere{}.

\subsection{Temporal trends}

We observed no evidence of a meaningful secular trend in reassessment timing.
In a supplementary Cox model with continuous de-identified year (M4 covariates), the HR per 5-year increase was \HRYearFive{}.
Figure~\ref{fig:yeartrend} displays unadjusted and M4-adjusted probabilities of 60-minute reassessment by policy era; both series fluctuated between approximately 7\% and 12\% without a consistent directional pattern.

\subsection{Post-analgesic pathway analysis}

In post-analgesic sensitivity analyses (time zero at first analgesic), cumulative reassessment curves by race/ethnicity largely overlapped (Figure~\ref{fig:postrx}).
The multivariate log-rank test was not statistically significant in the primary trauma pain-score-10 subgroup, suggesting that demographic differences in the primary analysis are not primarily driven by post-treatment reassessment intervals.
This supports interpreting insurance and race associations as operating earlier in the encounter or through pathways other than post-analgesic documentation alone.
